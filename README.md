# Retail Analytics Dashboard

An end to end data analyst project: a messy retail sales export is cleaned in Python, loaded into a relational SQLite database, analyzed with hand written SQL, and presented in an interactive Streamlit dashboard.

## Problem

A 10 store home and lifestyle retailer wants to understand two years of sales (2024 to 2025). Leadership is asking:

* How is revenue trending, and how strong is the holiday season?
* Which products, categories and regions drive the business?
* Who are the most valuable customers, and how concentrated is revenue among them?
* Can we trust the data? The raw export from the point of sale and CRM systems is known to be messy, and one store manager reported a system outage in February 2025.

## Data

The dataset is synthetic, generated by `src/generate_data.py` with a fixed random seed, so every run produces identical files. It is modeled on a realistic raw export.

| Table | Raw rows | Clean rows | Description |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| stores | 10 | 10 | 2 stores in each of 5 regions (Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Southwest, West) |
| products | 25 | 25 | 5 categories: Home Goods, Electronics, Apparel, Kitchen, Outdoor |
| customers | 520 | 505 | Name, email, signup date, state, region |
| orders | 19,769 | 19,769 | Jan 2024 to Dec 2025, status completed, refunded or cancelled |
| order_items | 35,164 | 34,419 | Product, quantity, unit price at sale, line revenue |

Data quality problems were injected on purpose so the cleaning step solves real issues:

* Three date formats mixed together (YYYY-MM-DD, MM/DD/YYYY, DD-Mon-YYYY), plus a few free text signup dates
* State values typed many ways (NY, ny, New York, and codes with stray spaces), 111 raw variants for 25 states
* About 7% of customer emails missing
* 15 near duplicate customers (the same person re-entered with different name casing)
* About 2% of line items with zero or negative quantity
* About 10% of prices stored as currency text such as $24.99 or $1,099.00
* About 4% of stored line revenue values computed before discounts
* A week long order outage at one Midwest store in February 2025

## Tools

* **Python** (pandas, numpy) for generation and cleaning
* **SQLite** via the standard library `sqlite3` module for storage and analysis
* **SQL**: CTEs, window functions (NTILE, rolling SUM with a ROWS frame, SUM OVER for shares), a recursive CTE date spine, LEFT JOINs to keep zero days
* **Streamlit** and **Plotly** for the dashboard

## Approach

1. **Generate** (`src/generate_data.py`): pure standard library Python builds the raw CSVs in `data/raw/` with seasonality (November and December lift, summer lift for Outdoor, weekend lift), 8% growth in 2025, and the injected quality issues.
2. **Clean** (`src/clean_data.py`): small, tested helper functions parse dates, normalize states, strip currency formatting and title case names. The pipeline then merges duplicate customers by normalized name plus state, keeps the earliest customer_id, remaps their orders, drops invalid quantities and recomputes line revenue. Every fix is counted in `data/processed/data_quality_report.md`.
3. **Load** (`src/build_database.py`): builds `data/retail.db` with primary keys, foreign keys, CHECK constraints and indexes on join and filter columns, then runs a foreign key integrity check.
4. **Analyze** (`sql/queries.sql`, `src/analysis.py`): six hand written queries cover the monthly trend, top products, regional performance, customer value segments, store level anomaly detection and order status mix. The script runs each query and prints a labeled summary.
5. **Visualize** (`app.py`): a Streamlit dashboard with region, status and date filters and four tabs: Overview, Products and Regions, Customers, Data Quality.

Revenue is defined as the sum of line revenue on completed orders. Refunded and cancelled orders are excluded.

## Key Findings

All numbers come from running the pipeline in this repo.

**Headline KPIs (completed orders, 2024 to 2025)**

* Total revenue: **$2,728,503**
* Completed orders: **17,395** (17,222 with at least one valid line item)
* Average order value: **$158.43**

**Growth and seasonality**

* Revenue grew **11.7%** year over year, from $1.29M in 2024 to $1.44M in 2025.
* November and December deliver **23.8%** of revenue in just 4 of 24 months. December 2025 was the peak month at $188,326; January 2024 was the slowest at $67,820.

**Products and categories**

* The Ultrabook Laptop alone is **12.0%** of revenue on only 316 units, so a small number of high ticket sales carry a large share of revenue.
* The top 10 of 25 products produce **56.4%** of revenue.
* Electronics is the largest category at **28.5%** of revenue, followed by Apparel (21.7%), Outdoor (18.6%), Kitchen (16.0%) and Home Goods (15.1%).

**Regions**

* The Midwest leads with **23.7%** of revenue and the highest average order value ($166.79). The Northeast trails at **16.7%**.

**Customers**

* The top third of customers by spend (169 people) generate **50.2%** of revenue, averaging $8,105 each, versus $2,543 for the bottom third. They also order more than twice as often (45.2 versus 19.2 orders).

**Order status**

* **12.0%** of orders were refunded (6.8%) or cancelled (5.2%), representing $361,570 of gross order value.

**Anomaly: store outage**

* Columbus Short North (S06, Midwest) recorded **1 order from Feb 10 to Feb 16, 2025**, about 7% of its normal 15.2 orders per week. No other store was flagged, which points to a point of sale or system outage rather than a demand drop. Those sales are likely unrecorded and should be recovered from backup logs or excluded when benchmarking that store.

**Data quality**

* 15 duplicate customers were merged and 475 of their orders were remapped to the surviving record.
* 4,358 order dates (22%) were not in ISO format and would have broken monthly grouping.
* 3,638 prices (10.3%) were stored as text; without cleaning, revenue sums would skip or fail on them.
* 745 line items with zero or negative quantity were dropped, and 538 stale line revenue values were recomputed.

## How to Run

The database and CSVs are committed, so the dashboard works right after cloning.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/rxj0102/retail-analytics-dashboard.git
cd retail-analytics-dashboard
python -m venv venv
source venv/bin/activate        # Windows: venv\Scripts\activate
pip install -r requirements.txt

streamlit run app.py
```

To rebuild everything from scratch:

```bash
python src/generate_data.py     # data/raw/*.csv
python src/clean_data.py        # data/processed/*.csv and data_quality_report.md
python src/build_database.py    # data/retail.db
python src/analysis.py          # prints the SQL analysis summary
python tests/test_cleaning.py   # runs the cleaning helper tests
```
